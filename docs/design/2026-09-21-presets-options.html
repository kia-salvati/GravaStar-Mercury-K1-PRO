Lighting presets — four structures on the Frosted-cards app

The Lighting screen of the app at apps/web: same tokens, glass recipe, rail and card grammar. A preset is a full snapshot — profile block (effect, per-effect brightness / speed / Colour Mixing), effect-colour block (one RGB per effect) and per-key block (126 × RGB for Custom). Applying writes all three and verifies each; it is the app's first write.

Every option is shown against the same mock: the board currently on Windmill B1 S0; presets stored per keyboard uuid, max 10. Use Stored to see the empty, mid and full states; Link decides whether Apply can write the colour blocks (on 2.4G they are not yet captured, so the profile is restored and the colours are skipped). Click a preset, then Apply / Rename / Delete; try Save current at 10 of 10 and Import with the cap in the way.

1 — Preset shelf

A horizontal strip of swatch-cards at the top of the Lighting screen, above the future live controls. The first card is always the action: "Save current" as a dashed card, which at 10 of 10 turns into a solid "10 of 10 — replace one" card. Selecting a card opens a detail card beneath the shelf with the full readout and Apply / Rename / Delete.

Structure

  • Shelf card = name, effect, brightness/speed as small step blocks, and the 40 px recogniser: one swatch, a mixed-hue swatch, or for Custom the 126-key dot grid (14 × 9) — at 40 px the grid is legible enough to tell "WASD red" from "rainbow rows", which three dominant-colour bands are not.
  • Detail card carries the saved date and the three actions; Apply opens the confirm sheet, then the result sheet with the keyboard's read-back per block.
  • Export / Import live in the shelf card's header as quiet buttons; Import over the cap opens a chooser that counts free slots and asks which incoming presets to keep.
  • The controls card below is a dashed "Live controls — Stage 2" placeholder; its place is fixed under the shelf.

Cap

  • Never hidden: the action card changes state and copy at 10. Choosing it enters replace mode: tapping any shelf card asks "Replace 'Night' with the current lighting?".

Cost at 480 px

  • Only three and a half cards are visible; the rest is horizontal scroll, so names past the fourth are out of sight until you scroll. The detail card takes ~150 px of height below the shelf, pushing the future controls down. At 900 all ten fit without scrolling.

2 — List with inline readouts

One card, a vertical list: swatch, name, effect and stages on one row each, saved date at the right, a count in the header. Tapping a row expands it in place (accordion) into the full readout plus Apply / Rename / Delete, so nothing moves elsewhere on the screen. The future controls card sits above the list — controls first, library second.

Structure

  • Row recogniser is the 18 px swatch; for Custom, three dominant-colour bands (top three colours by key count, proportional widths) — at 18 px a dot grid is noise, bands still say "mostly red with some cyan". The expanded row shows the full dot grid.
  • Header: "4 of 10" count and Save current; footer: Export all · Import. At 10 the count goes full-weight and Save current stays enabled but opens the replace chooser.
  • Empty state is a sentence with the one useful action, not a blank card.

Cap

  • The count is always on screen. Save current at 10 → "10 of 10. Replace one?" with the list itself becoming the target picker (rows show "Replace" on the right). Import at cap → the import sheet shows incoming presets with free slots counted and lets you untick.

Cost at 480 px

  • Ten rows at ~46 px plus header and footer is ~560 px: the list alone nearly fills the window, so with the controls card above it the presets start below the fold and you scroll to reach them. Expanding a row adds ~130 px. It is the densest option and the least glanceable — colour is one 18 px square per row.

3 — Bank of ten slots

Ten fixed slots in a 5 × 2 grid, like a hardware memory bank. Empty slots are visible and numbered, so the cap is the shape of the thing, not a rule that appears later. Save current is a mode: the empty slots light up as "Save here"; at 10 of 10 the full slots do, as "Replace". Selecting a filled slot shows its readout and actions in a detail area under the grid.

Structure

  • Slot = number, recogniser, name. Recogniser is the swatch, or for Custom the 126-key dot grid scaled to the slot (84 px wide at 900, the slot's width at 480) — the grid is the point of a bank: you recognise slot 4 by its pattern.
  • Detail area: full rows (effect, colour, stages, mixing, saved date) and Apply / Rename / Delete. Delete frees the slot; slots keep their numbers, so "slot 3" stays meaningful in the export file too.
  • Import maps incoming presets onto free slots in order; when there are not enough, the sheet says how many will not fit and offers to pick.

Cap

  • Intrinsic. There is no eleventh slot to hide; the full state reads as "all ten lit".

Cost at 480 px

  • Five slots across a ~400 px panel gives 72 px slots: names are ellipsised at about nine characters and the dot grid drops to roughly 3 px dots — pattern still readable, individual keys not. The grid plus detail area is ~330 px, leaving ~250 px for the future controls card without scrolling. Two rows of ten is the most compact full state of the four.

4 — Current vs saved

Two panes: what the keyboard reports now on the left, the selected preset on the right, with every differing value marked. A chooser strip of chips picks the preset. Apply is the only primary action and it reads as "make the right pane true"; the result sheet then shows the left pane catching up, block by block. Save current is the reverse arrow: snapshot the left pane into a new chip.

Structure

  • Panes are the Device screen's own rows (effect, colour, brightness, speed, mixing) plus the per-key grid. Rows that differ get an accent dot and value; a summary line under the panes counts them — "3 of 5 values and the per-key block change".
  • Chips carry only swatch + name; the readout lives in the pane, so ten chips wrap into two lines and stay small.
  • The dongle case is visible before applying: on 2.4G the colour and per-key rows are marked "won't write yet" in the saved pane, so the outcome is predicted, not discovered.

Cap

  • Count in the chooser; at 10 the Save current chip turns into "Replace…" and the chips become targets.

Cost at 480 px

  • Two panes across ~400 px are ~190 px each: labels shrink to 11.5 px and the per-key grid to 4 px dots. Ten chips take two rows (~70 px). Best for deciding whether to apply; worst for browsing, since only one preset's readout is visible at a time. At 900 the panes are roomy and the diff reads at a glance.
